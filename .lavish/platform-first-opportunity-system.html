Search order

Invert the funnel, preserve the controls.

Current · calendar-first
  1. Load a narrow set of official events.
  2. Require contract text to contain a known family and reference period.
  3. Require both venues to have a candidate before deeper analysis.
  4. Require auto-approved equivalence.
  5. Only then observe books and consider locked arbitrage.
Result: safe but sparse. It cannot see a platform-native event unless an existing calendar adapter already knows how to name it.
Target · platform-first
  1. Continuously enumerate venue events, nested markets, rules, timestamps, tags, and revisions.
  2. Classify what could move each contract: scheduled release, speech, live event, rolling news, or no discrete catalyst.
  3. Use official calendars and sourced news to corroborate or refine that hypothesis.
  4. Generate several opportunity types with an explicit strategy label.
  5. Apply that strategy’s own economics, model, risk, paper acceptance, and execution gate.
Result: high-recall discovery without pretending a forecast or convergence trade has a locked payoff.
Target architecture

One universe. Three meanings of opportunity.

The shared catalog eliminates repeated ingestion and matching work. Isolation begins at the router, before any risk or order decision.

Architecture intent. The current locked-arbitrage executor remains the authority for that lane; relative-value and directional trades do not inherit its name, thresholds, or permissions.

Strategy contract

Broaden the model by giving it lanes—not a vague mandate.

A single “trade score” across all three would make results impossible to attribute and risk impossible to control.

Build plan

Six vertical slices to an evidence-producing system.

Each slice produces a usable operator surface or dataset, rather than building disconnected layers that cannot prove value.

Venue catalog + cursor checkpoints

Normalize Polymarket events/markets and Kalshi events/live/historical markets. Persist raw payload hash, rules, parent event, series, listing/close times, tags, status, liquidity, and revision history.

  • Migrate Polymarket discovery to keyset cursors.
  • Use Kalshi event identity plus historical-market filters.
  • Make scans resumable and date-addressable.
Gate: repeated sync is complete, idempotent, and auditable

Platform-native catalyst intelligence

Classify every active contract without requiring a calendar hit. Produce catalyst_kind, entities, predicted time/window, horizon, confidence, and evidence fields.

  • Scheduled measurement or decision
  • Speech/hearing/debate/live event
  • Rolling/unscheduled or no discrete catalyst
Gate: reviewed recall by category and time horizon

Calendar and source enrichment

Join the platform-derived candidates to official calendars, venue metadata, and the existing sourced-news scanner. Enrichment may raise confidence or correct timing; its absence does not erase the market.

  • Preserve source URL and observed-at time.
  • Track conflicts instead of silently overwriting.
Gate: every catalyst time has provenance and confidence

Opportunity router + weekly board

Generate locked-arb, relative-value, and directional candidates into separate tables and dashboard sections. Show reason codes, required data, time-to-catalyst, liquidity, and whether the candidate is observable, paper-eligible, or blocked.

  • The first useful output is a ranked weekly opportunity map.
  • No strategy can borrow another strategy’s enablement.
Gate: every candidate has one explicit strategy contract

Forward event-window collector

Capture all selected venue contracts—not only pre-matched pairs—through scheduled/warm/hot/burst/cooldown windows. Record books, trades, source timestamps, receive timestamps, revisions, and catalyst links.

  • Creates honest training data while old archives are reconstructed.
  • Never turns price-only history into executable fill claims.
Gate: complete event windows and measured data loss

Independent paper acceptance

Replay each lane against its own baseline and loss model. Acceptance analytics run asynchronously and in parallel, outside the real-time monitoring, signal, and execution path.

  • Per lane: at least 50 catalyst windows and 200 intents; positive executable return at the 10% band; positive lower 95% event-clustered bound; controlled drawdown and concentration; stability across time and catalyst families.
  • Combined P&L remains visible for reporting but cannot promote a failed lane. Any bounded live pilot still requires explicit operator approval.
Gate: independent proof with zero hot-path latency

Decision 1 · approved

Shared discovery, isolated strategy lanes.

Recorded: one platform-first catalog feeds locked arbitrage, relative value, and directional catalyst trading. Each lane keeps its own admission rules, risk budget, ledger, acceptance evidence, enablement, and kill switch. Relative-value and directional trading remain non-live until independently accepted.
Decision 2 · approved

Forward shadow intents, scored from later books.

Recorded: relative-value and directional signals create immutable, timestamped shadow intents. Fillability, exits, P&L, drawdown, and calibration are scored only from later observed books. They create no exchange orders and do not assume the displayed entry price was fillable. Locked arbitrage continues under its existing paper controls.
Decision 3 · approved

Event-window market microstructure.

Recorded: directional v1 uses order-book imbalance, trade flow, spread and depth changes, cross-venue lead-lag, volatility, and time-to-catalyst. The catalyst clock schedules monitoring but never chooses YES or NO by itself. Real-time news interpretation and fundamental outcome forecasting are outside directional v1.
Decision 4 · approved

Machine-checkable structural relationships only.

Recorded: relative-value v1 admits the same underlying event with ordered thresholds, nested deadlines, mutually exclusive or exhaustive buckets, or cross-venue near-equivalents with an explicitly recorded settlement-basis difference. Every pair stores its invariant and residual basis risk. Semantic similarity may retrieve candidates but cannot authorize a shadow intent.
Decision 5 · approved

Short-horizon event reaction capture.

Recorded: directional intents are emitted only in hot or burst windows. Research evaluates 30-second, 2-minute, 10-minute, and 30-minute outcomes, while the executable shadow policy exits at the first of signal reversal, 10 minutes, hard stop, or catalyst cooldown. Directional v1 never holds to resolution by default.
Decision 6 · approved

Full catalog, bounded hot lane.

Recorded: every active and newly listed contract is continuously cataloged at low cadence. High-frequency book and trade sampling is reserved for liquid, strategy-relevant contracts with a catalyst inside seven days. Budget-excluded candidates remain visible with reason codes instead of disappearing.
Decision 7 · approved

Evidence-tiered monitoring cadence.

Recorded: corroborated event times may enter burst cadence; exact venue metadata may enter hot cadence without external corroboration; model-inferred times remain warm or scheduled until corroborated. Provenance, confidence, conflicts, and every lane promotion or demotion are persisted.
Decision 8 · approved

Unit return plus executable-capacity bands.

Recorded: every shadow intent is scored at one contract and at 5%, 10%, and 20% of later verified executable depth, subject to maximum notional. Unit return and capacity-weighted P&L are reported separately. Shadow balances do not compound and models cannot choose size in v1.
Decision 9 · approved

Per-lane proof authorizes promotion; pooled P&L remains visible.

Recorded: directional and relative-value lanes must independently pass the pre-registered sample, executable-return, confidence, drawdown, concentration, and stability gates. Combined P&L remains a dashboard and logging metric but cannot conceal a failed lane. Acceptance computation runs asynchronously and in parallel, outside the market-data, signal, shadow-intent, and future execution paths, so it cannot add reaction or order latency.
